Everyone else on the boat wanted manta rays, and to be fair, we got them — three, gliding past at a distance that made everyone's regulator breathing go audibly ragged with excitement. I was excited too. But the thing I actually remember, the thing I think about more, is a single parrotfish I found on the safety stop, scraping algae off a chunk of dead coral with a sound like someone grinding chalk, utterly uninterested in the eleven humans hovering nearby.

You can hear a parrotfish eat before you can see it. It's not subtle. And it turns out that sound — the actual, mundane business of a reef staying alive, one fish's lunch at a time — did something the mantas didn't, which was make the whole ecosystem feel less like a show being performed for us and more like a place that would keep existing exactly the same way whether we'd shown up or not.

I have worse photos of the parrotfish than the mantas. I don't need the photos to remember it.
