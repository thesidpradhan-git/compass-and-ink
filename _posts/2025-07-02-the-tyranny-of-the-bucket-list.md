---
title: "The Tyranny of the Bucket List"
categories: [essays]
excerpt: "A bucket list turns travel into a chore with good branding. I stopped keeping one and started noticing more."
tags: [ideas, planning, lists]
---

I kept a bucket list for about four years. Machu Picchu, the Northern Lights, some waterfall in Iceland I'd seen on someone else's feed. Crossing items off felt like progress, right up until I noticed I couldn't remember much about the crossing-off itself — just the fact of it, filed away like a completed form.

The problem with a bucket list isn't ambition. It's the framing: a list implies a finish line, and travel doesn't actually have one, which means the list can only ever produce two feelings — anxious incompletion or hollow completion, neither of which has much to do with why you wanted to go in the first place.

I stopped keeping one on purpose, somewhere around the fourth item that turned out to matter less than the unplanned thing that happened on the way to it. What I have instead is messier and harder to show anyone: a loose, unranked pile of things that seemed interesting when I heard about them, with no obligation to get to any particular one before I die. It's a worse system for bragging rights. It's a much better system for actually noticing where I am.
