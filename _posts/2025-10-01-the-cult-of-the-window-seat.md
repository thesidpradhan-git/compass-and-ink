There's a small, quietly fought war on every long-haul flight between window people and aisle people, and I've never once been tempted to switch sides. The window seat costs you legroom, easy bathroom access, and the goodwill of two strangers you'll have to climb over — and I'd pay all of it again, every time, for three hours over Greenland's ice sheet with nothing between me and it but a scratched acrylic pane.

Aisle people have the better argument, technically. Freedom of movement is a real thing to want on a ten-hour flight. But I think what window people are actually paying for isn't the view so much as the reminder — that below the recycled air and the seatback screen, an actual world is scrolling past at five hundred miles an hour, indifferent to the movie you're not watching.

I don't think this makes window people better travelers. It might make us slightly worse seatmates. But I've never once, in fifteen years of flying, regretted paying the aisle tax. I've regretted almost every flight I didn't get one.
