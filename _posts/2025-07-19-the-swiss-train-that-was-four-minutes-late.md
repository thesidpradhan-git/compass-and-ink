title: "The Swiss Train That Was Four Minutes Late"
categories: [travel]

The departure board said 14:07. At 14:11 it still said 14:07, and by then a small, quiet unrest had settled over the platform — not anger exactly, more a collective recalibration of a worldview. Swiss trains run on time the way gravity applies; a four-minute delay wasn't an inconvenience so much as a minor crisis of faith.

An older man beside me checked his watch three times in ninety seconds. A woman on a bench actually laughed, once, sharply, like she couldn't quite believe what she was witnessing. When the train finally pulled in — four minutes and forty seconds late, an intercom voice apologizing with what sounded like genuine institutional shame — everyone boarded in the exact same orderly silence as if nothing had happened at all.

I've been late for trains in six countries and never once seen a delay taken so personally. There's something almost moving about a system reliable enough that four minutes counts as an event. I got where I was going. I don't think anyone else on that platform quite forgave the railway by the time we arrived.
