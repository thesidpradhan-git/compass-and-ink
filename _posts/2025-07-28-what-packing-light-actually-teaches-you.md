For years I packed for a version of the trip that never actually happened — the hiking boots for the hike I didn't take, the formal shirt for the dinner that never materialized, the second camera lens I used exactly once in six trips. It took carrying all of it up four flights of a Lisbon walk-up to finally admit the extra weight wasn't preparation. It was anxiety, given a packing cube.

Going down to one bag forced a smaller, more honest question at every decision: not *might I need this* but *have I actually needed this before, on a real trip, not a hypothetical one*. Almost everything failed that test. What survived was a much shorter list, and packing it took eleven minutes instead of ninety.

The lighter bag didn't just change my back. It changed the shape of the trip itself — fewer contingencies packed for meant fewer contingencies subconsciously expected, which meant more attention left over for whatever the place actually offered instead of whatever I'd pre-decided it might require. I pack for the trip I'm taking now. It turns out that was always the only trip that mattered.
