Eleven meters from the riad, according to the blue dot, and I could not find the door. The medina doesn't run on street names so much as on landmarks that assume you already know them — "past the second dyer, before the spice stall with the good saffron" — and my phone, for all its confident GPS arithmetic, had no idea what a dyer was.

A boy of about nine watched me circle the same fifty meters for the third time and, without being asked, walked me straight to the door, refused a tip twice before accepting it, and vanished back into whatever errand I'd interrupted.

I've since learned this is less generosity than economy — kids in the medina know every foreigner circling in place is a foreigner about to be late for check-in, and lateness makes people generous. Still. Eleven meters, a working phone, a functioning map app, and I needed a nine-year-old to get me home. I've stopped trying to be embarrassed about it.
