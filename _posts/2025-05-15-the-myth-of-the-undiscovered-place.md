---
title: "The Myth of the Undiscovered Place"
categories: [essays]
excerpt: "Every place you've ever visited was discovered by someone before you got there. Usually thousands of someones."
tags: [ideas, tourism, language]
---

Travel writing has a favorite compliment, and it's "undiscovered" — the undiscovered beach, the undiscovered village, the undiscovered corner of a city everyone else has apparently gotten wrong. I've written some version of this sentence myself, and I want to take it back, because it's never once been true.

The places we call undiscovered are, without exception, places where people already live, have lived, and will keep living long after our flight home. What we actually mean is *undiscovered by people like us* — a phrase that's less charming once you say it plainly, since it quietly centers an entire category of traveler as the true discoverer and everyone already living there as scenery waiting to be found.

I don't think the impulse is malicious. It's mostly just excitement, badly worded. But language does the work of belief whether we intend it to or not, and "undiscovered" teaches a small, persistent lie: that a place doesn't fully exist until someone from outside notices it. The corrective isn't complicated. Just say what you mean — *new to me* — and let the place keep its own history intact, the one that was there the entire time you weren't looking.
