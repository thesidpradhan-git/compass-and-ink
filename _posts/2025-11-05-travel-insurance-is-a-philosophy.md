Nobody enjoys buying travel insurance. It's the least romantic line item in the entire trip, purchased in a tab you close as fast as possible, hoping never to think about it again. And I think that instinct — to buy it and immediately forget it — accidentally reveals something honest about how we actually relate to risk when we travel.

We plan trips as if the itinerary is the real version of events and anything that deviates from it is a malfunction. Insurance is the one moment in the whole process where you're forced to admit, in writing, with a dollar figure attached, that the malfunction is not just possible but statistically expected. Someone, on some percentage of trips, misses the connection, breaks the ankle, needs the hospital in a language they don't speak. Buying the policy is a small act of humility disguised as paperwork.

I don't think you need to dwell on this every time you travel. But I've started reading the policy summary instead of skimming past it, if only as a yearly reminder that the trip I'm planning and the trip I'll actually take are two different documents, and only one of them is fully up to me.
