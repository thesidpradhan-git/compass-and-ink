categories: [travel]
location: "Siem Reap, Cambodia"
excerpt: "Four hundred people stood in the dark waiting for the same photograph. I still don't regret being one of them."

We were told to arrive by 4:45am if we wanted a spot by the reflecting pool. We arrived at 4:40 and there were already three hundred people ahead of us, phone flashlights bobbing in the dark like a slow-moving constellation that had lost its way to the temple.

I'd read enough cynical trip reports to expect to hate this. A UNESCO site reduced to a photo op, four hundred strangers elbowing for the same reflection shot. And for the first twenty minutes, that's exactly what it was — muttered Mandarin, German, and Khmer all asking each other to move six inches to the left.

Then the sky did the thing skies do at Angkor, going from black to bruise-purple to the specific gold that makes the whole crowd go quiet at once, and every phone in the reflecting pool lifted in unison, and it stopped mattering that we were four hundred people taking the same picture. We were four hundred people who'd gotten up at 4am for the same reason, which turns out to be a kind of company.

I got my photo. It looks exactly like everyone else's. I don't mind.
