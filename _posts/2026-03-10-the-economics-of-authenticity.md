---
title: "The Economics of Authenticity"
categories: [essays]
excerpt: "The moment a local experience gets marketed as authentic, it's already started becoming something else."
tags: [ideas, tourism, economics]
---

Every destination eventually develops a menu of "authentic" experiences, and the word itself should be the tell — nothing that has to announce its authenticity is operating quite the way it did before someone decided to sell it. The home-cooked dinner with a local family. The traditional craft demonstration. The village that "still" does things the old way, italics doing a lot of quiet work in that sentence.

I don't think this makes these experiences fake, exactly. The family really does cook the meal. The craftsperson really does know the technique. But the moment either becomes a product with a price and a booking link, it's responding to a new set of incentives — what a paying visitor expects "authentic" to look like — which is a different thing from what the practice looked like before anyone was watching.

The honest version of this isn't to avoid the marketplace entirely, which is both impossible and a little self-righteous. It's to stop expecting the marketplace to deliver something outside itself. An authentic experience you paid for is still a real interaction with a real person — it's just also, unavoidably, a transaction shaped by what transactions shape. Both things are true. I've stopped needing the brochure to pretend otherwise.
