The power went at 8:40pm, mid-sentence, mid-meal, the whole block going dark at once with the specific suddenness that tells you it's not just your building. Nobody around me seemed surprised. Within ten minutes someone had dragged a speaker outside running on a car battery, someone else had produced dominoes, and a plastic table that hadn't been on the sidewalk an hour earlier was suddenly the center of the evening.

I asked my host how often this happened. She shrugged in a way that communicated "often enough that we have a system" more clearly than words would have. The system, as far as I could tell, was: candles for the tables that needed light, music for the tables that didn't, and an unspoken agreement that a blackout was mildly rude but not worth ruining an evening over.

Power came back a little after 11:30, and the street lights snapping on got a bigger cheer than anything else that happened all week — bigger than the music, bigger than the domino games. Everyone clapped for electricity like it had done something clever, then mostly kept sitting outside anyway.
