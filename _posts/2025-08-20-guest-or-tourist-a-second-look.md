---
title: "Guest or Tourist: A Second Look"
categories: [essays]
excerpt: "I've argued before that the line between guest and customer matters. A year later, I think I drew it too neatly."
tags: [ideas, hospitality, revisiting]
---

I wrote once that every place a tourist visits has an invisible line between being a guest and being a customer, and that crossing to the wrong side of it quietly degrades the whole premise of travel. I still think that's mostly right. I've come to think it's also a little too clean.

The truth is messier: most interactions on the road are both at once, guest and customer simultaneously, and treating that as a contradiction to resolve rather than a fact to accept was its own kind of naivety. The shopkeeper who invites you for tea is also, reasonably, hoping you'll buy something. That doesn't make the tea fake. It makes it exactly what hospitality has always been in places where hospitality and commerce were never as separated as my original argument assumed — an assumption, I'll admit, that came from a culture where the two are kept unusually far apart.

I'm not retracting the distinction. I'm retracting the idea that it's binary. Most of the best exchanges I've had abroad lived in the overlap, not on either side of the line, and I was slower than I should have been to notice I was the one who needed the line to be tidy.
