Jet lag has one upside, which is that it introduces you to a city's second shift. By 2am I'd given up on sleeping and gone looking for the Raohe night market, half expecting shuttered stalls and rolled-down grates. Instead I found maybe a third of the vendors still going, feeding taxi drivers, night-shift nurses, and one extremely committed group of teenagers who'd clearly decided sleep was for people with worse noodles at home.

The beef noodle soup guy didn't blink at a foreigner ordering at 2am with a phone translator. He just asked, in English better than my Mandarin, whether I wanted it spicy, and when I hesitated he made the decision for me and it was the correct one.

There's a version of a city that only exists after midnight, staffed by people who've made peace with running counter to everyone else's clock. I don't think you can plan to see it — you mostly just have to be awake and hungry at the wrong hour, which jet lag, for once, was generous enough to arrange.
