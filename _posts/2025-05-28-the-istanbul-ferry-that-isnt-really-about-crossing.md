You can cross continents in Istanbul for about thirty cents. The commuter ferry from Kabataş to Üsküdar takes fifteen minutes, runs every twenty, and is used almost entirely by people who are not thinking about the fact that they are moving from Europe to Asia — they're thinking about work, or the kid they need to pick up, or the tea getting cold in the little glass on the ferry's window ledge.

I rode it four times in one afternoon for no reason except that fifteen minutes felt like the right amount of time to sit still. Seagulls tracked the boat the entire way, close enough to hand-feed if you'd brought bread, which everyone around me had, like this was understood civic infrastructure rather than a novelty.

Nobody photographs the commuter ferry the way they photograph the Blue Mosque. It's not trying to be seen. That's exactly why it's the only place in the city where I felt like I was in Istanbul rather than at Istanbul, a distinction that took me three days and thirty cents to actually understand.
