Scotland's right to roam means you can pitch a tent almost anywhere that isn't someone's garden, which sounds like chaos until you actually try it and realize the chaos is entirely self-policing. Everyone I passed on the trail into Glen Affric was carrying out more rubbish than they'd carried in, out of what seemed like genuine collective agreement rather than posted signage, because there wasn't any.

I camped beside a loch that had no name I could find on any map, ate cold beans directly from the tin because I'd forgotten a pot, and listened to a stag roar somewhere across the water for most of an hour before realizing it wasn't going to stop and I should probably just sleep through it.

It rained sideways at 4am, briefly, the way it does there, and then didn't again. I've paid for hotel rooms with worse views than a wild pitch beside an unnamed loch, and none of them came with the specific, slightly unearned pride of a law that simply trusts you to behave.
