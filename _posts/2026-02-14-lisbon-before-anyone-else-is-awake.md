title: "Lisbon Before Anyone Else Is Awake"
categories: [travel]

excerpt: "The trams start before the cafés do, which leaves a strange forty-minute window that belongs only to people who couldn't sleep."

The 28 tram starts running at 6am, but the cafés along its route mostly open at 7, which leaves a window of about forty minutes where you can ride through Alfama past shuttered pastelarias with nowhere to actually stop, which is, it turns out, the entire point.

I hadn't planned to be up this early. Jet lag had other ideas, and by 5:45 I'd given up negotiating with it and gone looking for something to do with the extra hours. What I found was a city being quietly prepared rather than performed — a woman hosing down her doorstep, a delivery van double-parked with its hazards on, the tram driver nodding at regulars who didn't need to say where they were getting off.

Nobody was there for me to watch. That's the difference between this hour and every other one I'd spent in the city — at 8am Alfama becomes a set, mildly aware of its audience. At 6am it's just a neighborhood, doing what neighborhoods do before they remember they're famous. I got off at the last stop with nowhere open, and walked back the way I came, entirely satisfied with having seen nothing in particular.
